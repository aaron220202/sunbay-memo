ARecommendationPass · revisit at seed

A real problem and fast growth, but not yet a venture-scale company.

Sunbay solves a real problem for a buyer with budget: late payments cost European SMEs about €275B a year, and revenue is growing an estimated 15% a month across 20+ customers. But what it sells today, automated email and SMS reminders, is a feature Polish invoicing apps and banks already offer, and the KSeF e-invoicing mandate now gives them the same invoice data. The part that could make it a company, an AI agent that negotiates payment, appears in its funding announcement but not on its product pages or in its case studies, and in my model it is the belief that moves the return most. $6M post is a fair market price but the wrong price for us: our ~13% dilutes to ~7%, returning the fund needs a ~$1B exit, and at my odds the expected return is 2.2x against the 4.4x we need, a bar this deal clears only at about $3M post. I would lead the seed once the voice agent shows results with customers, average contracts pass €700 a month and net revenue retention is above 110%.

BKey arguments and risks

A good product with a low ceiling.

Why we could invest

  1. The ROI is obvious. One customer reports saving 40+ hours a week. At €290 a month, Sunbay charges roughly a tenth of the labour it replaces (my estimate, at €15 an hour).
  2. It is growing at a venture pace. ARR rose from ~$16K to ~$110K in 14 months (my estimate), about 15% a month, the bottom of the band investors want before a Series A.
  3. CEE is under-served. Upflow, Kolleno and Tesorio price for mid-market and up; the cheap UK tools plug into Xero, not Polish ERPs.
  4. KSeF is a free data pipe. Since April 2026 every Polish B2B invoice is structured, real-time data, so setup can shrink to one connection.
  5. The team learns fast. It narrowed a vague automation platform to collections and added Kogito, s20 and Kahoot's CTO as backers.
Upside case 17–26x
Sunbay becomes the AI collections clerk for Europe's mid-market, phoning debtors in their language and charging on cash recovered. Sold through accounting offices, the way Visma's SaldeoSMART reached 55,000 companies via 1,500 of them, that is €30–50M ARR and a $200–300M exit.

Key risks

  1. Today's product is a feature. Fakturownia already sends automatic SMS reminders after the due date; wFirma and PKO BP offer soft collection.build what they won't: voice negotiation, payment plans, disputes.
  2. KSeF helps rivals as much as Sunbay. Every Polish accounting app now sees the same receivables in real time.compete on outcomes and cross-border collection, not data access.
  3. SMB collections has a low ceiling. Chaser, the UK leader, is at roughly $3M revenue after 12 years.move up to 50–250 staff firms and price on cash recovered.
  4. The one feature that matters is unproven. The June 2026 announcement claims multilingual AI voice calls, but the product pages and case studies don't show them.make customer results from voice a condition of any cheque.
Downside case 0–1x
Sunbay stays a useful €1–3M ARR tool for Polish SMBs, gets squeezed by free reminders and sells for $5–20M behind later investors' liquidation preferences.
C1TeamStrong fitNo seller

The right founders for the problem. Nobody owns selling yet.

  • They are their own customer. The CEO has 9 years in ops and automation, including YC-backed Plane; the COO 5 years in finance ops at Droppe; the CTO 5 years engineering at mBank. The deck describes the pain with insider precision.
  • Ownership is clean and aligned. The Polish company register shows Sunbay sp. z o.o. incorporated in Warsaw on 19 March 2025, with all three founders as board members and the only registered shareholders.
  • Commitment came late. Two founders went full-time in April–May 2025, and the CTO's start date read "[SOON]". I would confirm vesting, the equity split and the CTO's start before any cheque.
  • Nobody owns sales. SMB software lives on cheap, repeatable acquisition, and none of the founders has built a sales engine. It is the first hire I would push for.
  • The "generalist" streak cuts both ways. It made the fast pivot into collections possible. It also produced a roadmap of side products before collections worked.
C2ProductUnproven core

The voice agent is the company, and it is unproven.

  • What's live is table stakes. Email and SMS sequences, statutory interest notes, OCR and bulk invoicing are useful and easy to copy.
  • Voice would turn a reminder tool into a clerk. The funding announcement claims multilingual AI calls, yet the product pages describe calls as tasks for staff. The voice itself is cheap, about $0.08–0.10 a minute on ElevenLabs, so the moat must be the negotiation logic and payment data around each call.
  • KSeF is missing from the deck. It became mandatory for all Polish firms in April 2026, and for micro firms in January 2027. Built natively on it, Sunbay could be the fastest collections tool to deploy in Poland.
  • What I would tell the founders: narrow further and charge more. Drop the document-request and contract-renewal roadmap, charge a share of cash recovered instead of a flat €290 a month, and sell through accounting offices.
C3Market€4.3B in the EU€181M in Poland

Big enough in Europe. Too small in Poland.

  • The pain won't be regulated away. About 11% of European revenue is paid late, and the EU's proposed 30-day payment cap was withdrawn after member states couldn't agree.
  • Buyers already want AI here. 59% of European firms use AI in payments management, so Sunbay has to win this market, not educate it.
  • Poland alone tops out near €180M. A fund-returner has to win abroad early. Customers already collect from debtors in 30 markets, which helps.
  • The realistic five-year case is about €8M ARR. Enough for a strategic sale; a fund-returner needs over 40,000 customers at today's prices. Build it yourself below.

C4CompetitionSqueezed from below

The real rival is the invoicing app the customer already pays for.

  • The deck picks the wrong rivals. It benchmarks against Tesorio, Collbox, Zapier and Make. In Poland the threat is the invoicing app or bank the customer already uses: Fakturownia already chases overdue invoices by SMS automatically.
  • Big outcomes here came from enterprise. Billtrust sold for $1.7B on enterprise billing and payments; no SMB reminder tool I could find has come close.
  • AI collectors are one pivot away. Murphy ($15M, backed by ElevenLabs) and JustSolve (€3.7M) chase consumer debt today. B2B is a short step.
  • The open space is CEE-native AI collection. Nobody clearly occupies it yet. Sunbay's only durable edge is getting there first, with real payment data.
  • My prediction: the window closes by the end of 2027. By then I expect a Polish invoicing app or bank to ship AI collection agents built on KSeF data.

C5TractionFastTiny base

Growing fast from a tiny base, and measuring the wrong thing.

  • Growth is genuinely strong. ARR went from ~$16K in April 2025 to ~$110K by June 2026 (my estimate from 20+ customers at €300–500 a month), about 13–15% a month, at the bottom of the 15–20% band cited for companies between seed and Series A.
  • But the base is tiny, and services paid the bills. In April 2025 consulting brought in ~$3K a month, twice the software revenue. Today's ARR is about a tenth of the $1M where Series A conversations start.
  • The deck measures activity, not money. "Automated actions" shows the product runs. A Series A investor will ask for net revenue retention (100% is the bar, 110%+ is good) and euros collected per customer.
  • Even strong growth slows. If monthly growth fades 30% a year, roughly the T2D3 shape, Sunbay reaches $1M ARR in early 2028 and about €10M in early 2034: a strategic-sale company.
  • The reference customers are friendly startups. Tequipy, the main case study, is itself VC-backed. A win with a traditional logistics or energy firm would prove more.

C6The round and returnsFair priceWrong price for us

The price is fine. The ceiling is the problem.

  • A fair market price, but the wrong price for us. The $4.8M pre-money sits below PitchBook's €6M median for European pre-seed and seed. But expected return falls in step with valuation, and at my odds the deal clears our 4.4x hurdle only at about $3M post, half the ask.
  • If we invested: $800K for 13.3%, with $800K held back for the seed. The full $1.2M (20%) would put too much of the fund behind a thesis I rate below hurdle. Diligence flag: Sunbay raised €550K in June 2026, yet the register lists only the founders as shareholders, which suggests a convertible whose terms would dilute us.
  • Returning the fund needs a ~$1B exit. Three more rounds at ~18% and a 5% option-pool top-up take us from 13.3% to ~7%. At 5x revenue, $1B means ~$200M ARR, or roughly 40,000 customers at today's blended prices; private software deals priced at 3.1–4.0x revenue in 2026.
  • For a fund-making outcome, all of this must hold: a voice agent that measurably cuts DSO, €8–10K contracts, distribution through accounting offices, net retention above 110%, real revenue outside Poland by year three, and a buyer paying 5x+ ARR. Short of that, the realistic win is a sale to Visma or a Polish bank within 3–4 years for $20–60M: roughly 2–5x for us.
  • What I would offer instead: a milestone-gated option. $200K on a $6M-cap SAFE (3.3%) plus a right to lead the seed, triggered by the three milestones in section A within 12 months. Sequoia used the same structure for YouTube: $1M up front, $4M once milestones were hit.

How the model works, and the deal assumptions

If the voice agent does not prove itself, Sunbay stays a reminder tool: 75% it shuts down, 25% it is bought for a small sum. If it works but incumbents launch AI collections first, it is squeezed (40% shut down, 35% small sale, 25% strategic sale). If it works and stays ahead, pricing power and international growth decide the size: both lead to a European leader (65%) or a fund-returner (35%); one leads mostly to a strategic sale; neither to a small or strategic sale. Exits: small sale $15M, strategic sale $40–80M depending on the buyer multiple, European leader $300M, fund-returner $1B. The hurdle assumes 2% annual fees for 10 years and 20% carry.
